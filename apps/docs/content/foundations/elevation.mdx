Elevation tells people how the layers of an interface stack: what is the page, what can be picked up, and what is
floating above everything else. Each level is a pair of tokens, a surface color and a shadow, that move together.

## Applying elevations

Interlink has four levels. Pick the lowest level that does the job; every step up asks for more attention.

<ElevationStack />

### Elevations in dark theme

In dark mode, higher surfaces get lighter and shadows soften. The surface does most of the work, which is why the
pairing rule below matters.

## Levels

### Sunken

A recessed backdrop for grouping, such as the columns of a board. It holds content; it is never interactive itself.

<SunkenVsNeutral />

### Default, raised and overlay

The default surface is the page. Raised surfaces hold cards people can open or move. Overlay surfaces hold
anything that floats above the page: menus, popups, dialogs and floating toolbars.

### Overflow

The overflow shadow sits under sticky headers and scroll containers to show there is more content beneath.

## States

Raised and overlay surfaces have hovered and pressed variants. Dragged items rise to the overlay level.
