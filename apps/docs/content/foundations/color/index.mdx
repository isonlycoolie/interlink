Interlink color is built for long working sessions: calm neutrals for most of the screen, and saturated color
saved for actions, status and moments that need attention. Every color in a product comes from a design token, so
light, dark and increased-contrast themes stay correct without extra work.

## Color anatomy

### Saturated colors

Ten hues give status and emphasis their meaning. Each ramp runs from a soft tint to a deep shade, tuned so the
steps used for text and icons meet contrast on Interlink surfaces.

### Neutral colors

Neutrals do most of the work: surfaces, text, borders and quiet controls. Light and dark themes each have their own
neutral ramp so contrast feels equal in both.

<PaletteRamps ramps="Neutral,DarkNeutral" />

### Alpha colors

Translucent neutrals sit on any surface and keep their relationship to it, which is why hover and pressed
backgrounds use them.

## Applying color with design tokens

Never use a palette value directly. Tokens name the job a color does (`color.text.subtle`,
`color.background.danger.bold`) and resolve to the right palette step for the active theme.

## Emphasis levels

Roles come in emphasis steps from subtlest to boldest. Use subtle steps for large areas and bold steps for small,
important elements such as primary buttons.

### Use inverse tokens on bold backgrounds

Text and icons on bold backgrounds use the inverse tokens. Interlink's `Text` and `Heading` pick them for you when
they sit inside a `Box` with a bold background.

<InverseOnBold />

## Interaction states

Interactive backgrounds have hovered and pressed tokens. Layer them on top of the current state instead of
inventing new colors, and keep selected and focused states visually distinct from each other.

<InteractionStates />

## Accessibility in color

Body text meets a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, and large text, icons and focus rings at least 3:1, in every
theme. Never rely on color alone: pair status color with an icon, a label or both.

## Designing in dark mode

Dark mode is a theme, not an inversion. Surfaces get lighter as they rise, shadows soften, and saturated colors
shift toward lighter steps to keep contrast. Using tokens is all it takes to support it.
